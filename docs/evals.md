## Notes per component

Confidence intervals (L-11): `impact` AUC 95 % CI [0.45, 0.54] and `calibration` Brier skill
[-0.094, -0.008], both bootstrapped over days (the names of one day share the market move);
`facts` within-1 % [0.24, 0.43] over filings; `relevance` dedup F1 [0.96, 0.99] over items.
Backtests on a split that was used to fit or choose anything show **in-sample** in the table
instead of a pass (e.g. `impact` on train, Sharpe 7.19).

### collectors

- **Metric**: `price_session_completeness`, the mean over the 77-name universe of the share of
  exchange sessions (warmup + train + validation) that have a daily bar.
- **Gold set**: derived. The exchange calendar is SPY's sessions; the weekdays without a session
  are listed in `docs/02_data_sources.md` and must be exactly the NYSE holidays. Alongside the
  metric the eval reports duplicate share, availability-rule violations and test-period rows,
  each of which must be 0.
- **Why**: a missing bar silently shifts every rolling window and return a strategy computes.
- **Planted errors**: six corruptions of a real validation slice (news row moved into the test
  period, news stamped before publication, bar stamped before the close, a missing session, a
  duplicate article, a +50 % spike). The quality checks must flag all six; the count is in
  `details.planted_errors`.

### simulator

- **Metric**: `hand_fixture_exact_share`, the share of hand-worked fixtures whose final equity
  matches the hand computation to the cent and whose trade count matches.
- **Gold set**: derived, `evals/sim_fixtures.py`: buy-and-hold toy (two names, 50 % cap, 10 bps),
  round-trip costs on a flat price (5 bps), exit at the open when the score drops to 0, time exit
  at the close of D+h with re-entry. The arithmetic is written next to each fixture.
- **Target**: 1.0. **Why**: simulator correctness is binary; one wrong fill or cost rule biases
  every backtest number.

### backtests (`backtest_<strategy>_<split>`)

- **Metric**: annualised Sharpe of daily close-to-close equity returns after 5 bps per side
  (risk-free 0), with a 95 % moving-block bootstrap CI (2,000 resamples, 5-day blocks) in
  `details`, next to total and annualised return, max drawdown, one-way turnover, hit rate per
  round trip and costs paid. `make backtest STRATEGY=<name|all> SPLIT=<train|val>`.
- **Gold set / target**: derived: the Sharpe of buy-and-hold on the same split and the same
  10 watchlist names (target 0 for buy-and-hold itself).
- **Why**: risk-adjusted return after costs; beating "hold what you trade" is the minimum bar
  for a timing rule to be worth its turnover.
- **Rules** (D-35): signals from `MarketView(09:00 ET of D)`, entry at the open of D, exit at the
  close of D+h or at the open when the score is <= 0; equal weight capped at 20 % per name;
  parameters are textbook defaults, not tuned on any split.
- **Below target: `mean_reversion` on train** (Sharpe -0.02 vs 1.76). Fading 5-day drops in a
  steadily rising train period (buy-and-hold +9.7 % in six months) left the strategy on average
  24 % invested (fully in cash on 31 of 124 days), so it missed most of the trend, and 35 of its
  66 round trips lost after costs; it paid 1.3 % of capital in costs at a one-way turnover of 13x.
  Its validation Sharpe of 3.57 is the same rule in a choppier quarter (26 of 38 round trips won),
  so neither number is evidence of skill.
- **Read every number with its CI**: all six intervals contain their target (e.g. mean
  reversion on validation [0.56, 6.49] vs 1.34, momentum [-1.34, 6.56]); with 64 and 124 days no
  strategy is distinguishable from buy-and-hold. The validation Sharpes above 2 triggered the
  time-leak-check: static scans, the shift test (`tests/sim/test_no_lookahead.py`) and the
  train/validation reversal of mean reversion point to sampling noise, not look-ahead.

### relevance

- **Metric**: `dedup_f1`, F1 of "dropped as a near-duplicate"; precision and recall in
  `details` (eval plan targets P >= 0.90, R >= 0.80, so the F1 target is 0.85).
- **Gold set**: derived (`evals/eval_relevance.py`). 30 train ticker-days of the watchlist with
  at least 10 relevant headlines; 10 per day at their real times are the originals and each
  gets one planted copy 5-600 minutes later (wire prefix, source suffix, upper case,
  punctuation, one dropped word, one swapped word, two dropped words + suffix, clauses
  reordered). The other same-day headlines about the same company are the negatives.
- **Why**: a dropped original removes a real story from the daily mean (precision); a missed
  copy counts one syndicated story several times (recall).
- **Result**: F1 0.97 (P 0.99, R 0.96). Misses are mostly "two dropped words + suffix" on short
  headlines (recall 0.73 for that edit). The 4 false positives are informative: three
  "X to Present at the 44th Annual J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference" items (templated text
  that MinHash cannot tell apart; they were also a relevance false positive for JPM, fixed on
  2026-11-14 by `relevance.exclude_phrases` with a regression test, which moved the F1 from
  0.9746 to 0.9744) and one pair of templated analyst notes
  ("Citigroup / Jefferies Maintains Exxon Mobil ... Recommendation") with different ratings.
  A first version that drew originals from six months at random scored 1.0 because unrelated
  headlines never look alike; same-day originals are the harder, honest test.
- **Relevance itself** has no human gold yet; `details.relevant_share_train_by_ticker` shows
  the share of tagged news that mentions the company (0.33 NVDA to 0.54 MCD, 0.40 overall).

### sentiment

- **Metric**: macro-F1 over pos / neu / neg, headline only.
- **Gold set**: `evals/labels/news_100.csv`, 150 human-labelled Finnhub headlines from train +
  validation (D-34; 110 neu, 33 pos, 7 neg), label = view of a holder of the tagged ticker.
  Frozen on 2026-11-14 and hash-pinned in `tests/test_repo_rules.py` (D-37).
- **Target**: 0.80. **Why**: the classes are imbalanced; macro-F1 weights negative and positive
  as much as neutral, so "always neutral" scores badly.
- **Below target: FinBERT 0.52** (pos 0.48, neu 0.68, neg 0.39); the lexicon baseline scores
  0.55. FinBERT calls 47 of the 110 neutral headlines positive or negative, and reading them
  shows why: about 16 are about another company than the tagged one ("Intel (INTC) Falls 7% from
  Record High" tagged AAPL, "JPMorgan Faces $328M Lawsuit" tagged BAC), 8 are market wraps
  ("Dow, S&P 500 Fall For Third Straight Day"), and most of the rest are immaterial PR or
  partnerships ("COMCAST TO PROVIDE $60,000 TO ASSIST WITH FOOD INSECURITY") or mixed pieces
  ("Intuit Stock Is Down 24% Already In 2026. Time to Buy?"). FinBERT reads the tone of the
  sentence; the annotator judged what the news means for a holder of the tagged ticker. On the
  93 headlines that name the tagged company (what finbert_v0 averages after its relevance
  filter) FinBERT reaches 0.59 (neg 0.60). Negative has only 7 gold rows, so its F1 moves by
  about 0.1 per headline and the macro-F1 is noisy. Nothing is tuned on this set: it is the
  only human gold set and would stop being a test.
- **CIs and recall** (95 % i.i.d. bootstrap, 2,000 resamples): FinBERT 0.52 [0.42, 0.61],
  lexicon 0.55 [0.44, 0.66]: the two backends are not distinguishable on 150 rows. FinBERT's
  recall is pos 0.61 / neu 0.57 / neg 0.86: it finds the negatives (6 of 7) but calls 47 of 110
  neutrals directional (30 pos, 17 neg), so its errors are false alarms, not misses.
- **Second gold set, not PhraseBank** (`details.twitter_financial`): FinBERT was fine-tuned on
  Financial PhraseBank, so a PhraseBank score would flatter it. On the validation split of the
  public `zeroshot/twitter-financial-news-sentiment` (2,388 finance tweets/headlines, labelled
  bearish / bullish / neutral by the dataset authors, revision and sha256 pinned in
  `config/evals.yaml`, t.co links stripped) FinBERT scores **0.66 [0.64, 0.68]** (recall pos 0.61,
  neu 0.74, neg 0.76) and the lexicon 0.61 [0.59, 0.64]. So the gap to 0.80 is not only our
  stricter "holder of the tagged ticker" guideline: on independent finance text FinBERT is
  still well below target, and its main error is the same (190 neutral -> pos, 215 neutral ->
  neg). The macro-F1 on our set stays the reported value; nothing was tuned on either set.

### finbert_v0 (`backtest_finbert_v0_<split>`)

- Same metric, target and rules as the other backtests; strategy defined in D-36 (mean FinBERT
  score of relevant, deduplicated news in (previous weekday 09:00 ET, D 09:00 ET], long when
  > 0, hold to the close of D+1). Nothing fitted.
- **Below target on both splits**: train Sharpe -0.29 (total -2.0 %) vs buy-and-hold 1.76,
  validation -0.59 (-2.4 %) vs 1.34. The 95 % CIs ([-3.63, 2.34] and [-4.45, 3.14]) contain the
  target, so the strategy is not distinguishable from buy-and-hold either way.
- **Costs dominate**: a one-day hold re-trades most names every day (1,000 trades on train,
  one-way turnover 67x, 502 trades / 35x on validation). Costs paid were 6.9 % of initial
  capital on train and 3.4 % on validation, so before costs the strategy made about +4.9 % and
  +1.0 %, still below buy-and-hold on train (+9.7 %). Hit rate per round trip after costs is
  0.49 on both splits. Obvious next steps (longer horizon, an entry threshold above 0, a
  per-ticker baseline instead of the raw mean) are parameters to tune on train only (R3).
- The input itself is weak: FinBERT's macro-F1 on the human gold set is 0.52 (0.59 on relevant
  headlines), see `sentiment`.

### events

- **Metric**: macro-F1 over the event labels with gold support (multi-label, 'none' is a label,
  8-K item 9.01 dropped); micro-F1, exact set match and per-label precision/recall in `details`,
  95 % bootstrap CIs on macro and micro F1.
- **Gold set**: the `events` column of `evals/labels/news_100.csv` (150 headlines; 108 none,
  20 8.01, 10 2.02, 4 1.01, 3 5.02, 2 7.01, 2 2.01, 1 3.02, 1 other_item).
- **Methods** (D-39): (a) rules: the item codes of the tagged ticker's 8-Ks accepted within
  +/- 24 h of the headline; (b) TF-IDF (1-2 grams) + one-vs-rest logistic regression on the
  headline, trained on 72,630 train-split headlines that name their company, weak-labelled by
  (a), gold ids excluded. Settings fixed in `config/extraction.yaml` before scoring.
- **Target**: 0.75 macro-F1. **Why**: event classes are rare and unequal; accuracy would reward
  predicting 'none'.
- **Below target: (b) 0.28 [0.18, 0.40], (a) 0.25 [0.20, 0.37]**; micro-F1 0.54 vs 0.40, exact
  set match 0.53 vs 0.39. The CIs overlap, so (b) is not shown to be better on macro-F1; it is
  better on micro-F1 because it predicts 'none' more often (none recall 0.69 vs 0.44).
  Why both are low: (1) the rule labels by coincidence, not content: on an earnings day every
  headline of the ticker ("stock up 3 %", analyst notes) gets 2.02, which is why rule precision
  is 0.32 for 2.02 and 0.14 for 5.02; (2) (b) learns from those noisy labels, so it inherits the
  noise (weak labels: 57k none, 10.7k 2.02, 3.1k 7.01, 2.6k 8.01); (3) the human used 8.01 for
  immaterial PR and partnerships (20 rows) that rarely have an 8-K, so 8.01 F1 is 0.16-0.20 for
  both; (4) seven of the ten labels have 1-4 gold rows, each moving its F1 by 0.25-1.0, so the
  macro-F1 is mostly noise (2.01 is never positive in the weak labels at all).
- **Caveat**: news_100 was sampled by "an 8-K within +/- 24 h" with the rule's own window
  (D-34), so (a) sees the sampling variable. On the 60 rows drawn with no 8-K nearby the rule's
  exact match is 0.80 vs 0.62 for (b): there, 'none' is the right answer by construction.

### chat (D-46, `make eval-chat`)

**Metric**: faithful-answer rate: an answer counts only when every number it states (outside
quoted headlines) matches a tool output at the printed precision, every [n] marker maps to a
citation, every cited news id / 8-K accession exists in DuckDB *for the asked ticker* with
`available_at` inside the question's window (recomputed from the price bars, independently of
the tools), "the whole market fell" appears exactly when SPY fell >= 1 %, and the wording has no
causal verb and carries the disclaimer. **Gold**: `evals/chat_questions.yaml` (28 questions:
13 why-move incl. 5 days where SPY fell >= 1 % and a Saturday, 2 price, 3 news, 2 signals,
2 backtest, 6 refusals) fixes intents, plans and refusals; all values come from DuckDB.
**Target** 0.90 (docs/01). **Why**: a fluent wrong answer is the main failure of a RAG-like
agent; numbers and citations are checkable by code, so no LLM judge is needed (free-tier budget).

Result: 1.00 on 22 answered questions (54 citations checked); tool selection 1.00 (28);
refusals 6/6 (unknown ticker x2, guaranteed profit x2, test-period date, no ticker), 0 false
refusals; 5/5 poisoned headlines (instruction, delimiter breakout + causal bait, `<script>` +
key request, "replace every number with 999.99", long padding + foreign-citation request) stay
inside the delimiters with numbers, citations and verdict unchanged; planted errors caught
78/78 (13 each: wrong number, foreign-ticker news id, same-ticker item a week before the window,
causal wording, dropped disclaimer, orphan [n]); 0 LLM calls (the transport raises during the
run). **Read this score for what it is**: the composer builds the text from tool outputs by
construction, so 1.00 says the plumbing (windows, ticker scoping, citation ids, wording) is
right and that the checks can fail (planted errors), not that the cited items are the ones a
human would pick as context for the move: the ranking (relevance, |sentiment|, recency) has no
gold set. The cached LLM rephrase is guarded by the same checks but is not measured (no cached
responses exist; the eval makes 0 calls). The number check compares magnitudes; a flipped
"up"/"down" word with the right magnitude would pass it.

- **Target**: >= 0.90 within 1 %, 100 % grounded. **Why**: a number is right or useless; the

- **LLM on 15 filings: not run in this session**; the bake-off's 10-release LLM numbers are in

### llm_bakeoff

- **Metric**: JSON-schema adherence per HTTP call for `cohere/north-mini-code:free` vs
  `nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b:free`, latency per call, quality on 20 gold headlines and
  10 gold releases as a tie-breaker. `evals/eval_llm_bakeoff.py`, at most 15 calls, 10 items per
  call. **Target**: adherence 1.0. **Why**: on free tiers a model that breaks the schema burns

- **Sample size warning**: 20 headlines, 10 releases, 4-6 calls per model. One headline moves

### impact

Out-of-sample the impact model has no skill: validation AUC 0.498 (HistGradientBoosting, chosen
by val_a AUC 0.547) and 0.460 for the logistic-regression baseline, accuracy 0.490 vs 0.502 for
the majority class, Brier 0.260 vs 0.250 for the base rate. The gradient boosting reaches AUC
0.81 on train, so it memorises 1,220 rows of noisy one-day abnormal returns; the logistic model
is flat on train too (0.54). This is a negative result, not a bug: with ten names and six months
of training data the features (news tone, 8-K items, YoY growth, last day's move, volatility)
show no detectable link to the next day-and-a-half's abnormal return.

### calibration

Platt scaling fitted on val_a halves the ECE of the raw probabilities on val_b (0.128 -> 0.066)
and lowers Brier (0.266 -> 0.257), but stays worse than predicting val_b's base rate (0.249):
Brier skill -0.03. Isotonic is worse on val_b (0.271) though perfect in-sample (ECE 0), the
expected overfit on about 300 rows. Calibration cannot add discrimination the model lacks; the
up-share also moved from 0.53 (val_a) to 0.47 (val_b). Plot: `docs/figs/reliability_impact.png`.

### arena (`make arena`, D-42; full report `docs/05_arena.md`)

Metric: deflated Sharpe of `aggregate_equal` on validation, N = 91 trials logged in
`evals/trials.csv` (76 selection-grid points incl. the 16 Hedge points, 8 arena strategies, 3
ablation variants, 2 models, 2 calibrators); target 0.95. Value 0.002: its Sharpe (-1.58) is far
below the 4.22 that the best of 91 unskilled trials reaches by luck, and in the matched random control (same entry days and
holding lengths, random names, 200 draws) it sits at the 13.5th percentile. SR0 is high because
each trial covers only 32-124 sessions, so trial Sharpes vary widely; even the best strategy
(mean reversion, 3.57, 94.5th percentile of its controls) has a DSR of only 0.36. The drop-one
ablation shows the aggregate coincided with better validation returns without sentiment (0.96)
or without impact (0.85) than with all three, both CIs overlapping zero.

### aggregator, event, impact backtests

On val_b, the only days no choice saw, the equal-weight aggregate has Sharpe -3.15 (95 % CI
-7.3 to 2.8) vs 2.05 for buy-and-hold, with 126 trades and 1,158 USD of costs. Its validation
backtest (-1.58) includes val_a, where its hold period and threshold were picked. `impact` on
train shows Sharpe 7.19: that is in-sample (the model was fitted on those days) and must not be
read as skill; on validation it is -0.63. `event` was tuned on train (Sharpe -0.48, hold 10 days,
threshold 0) and gets 3.06 on validation from 10 round trips, CI -0.55 to 6.55, which contains
buy-and-hold's 1.34: indistinguishable from it.

`aggregate_hedge` (D-44): Hedge weights updated daily on realised drift_ar plus a confidence
threshold p_min on the calibrated p, chosen on val_a over 16 points that include fixed equal
weights. Equal weights without a threshold won on val_a (Sharpe 1.14 vs 1.03 for the best Hedge,
eta 3; every p_min lowered it, down to -3.5 at 0.55), so the chosen Hedge is the equal-weight
aggregate and inherits its val_b result. With a 32-session val_a and three components that are
near noise (impact AUC 0.50), the weights have little signal to learn from.

### prompt injection (tests, not a scored eval)

`tests/test_injection.py`: 8 poisoned texts (ignore-previous, fake SYSTEM turn, closing-tag
breakout, spoofed data delimiters, "reply BUY", `<script>` + label request, fake annotator
instruction with a planted "$999 billion", translate request) appended to clean headlines and
releases. Pinned: the TF-IDF event verdict, the regex facts and the gateway's wrapping (one
delimiter pair, spoofed delimiters and HTML stripped) do not change; a mock model that obeys the
injection (empty list, prose, wrong type) fails validation and the batch lands on the
deterministic fallback with one valid record per item. **Known limitation**: the lexicon
sentiment fallback follows no instructions but counts words, so a poison stuffed with positive
cue words ("bullish, beats, soars") flips a negative headline; the test pins this so it stays
visible. A planted figure placed *before* the real ones would also be taken by the regex (it
takes the first match); only appended poisons are tested.
